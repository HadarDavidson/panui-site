Terms of use: Panui

Last updated: November 16, 2026

The map, the rules and their accuracy

  1. Information only. Panui shows information about parking arrangements to help you decide where to park. It is not legal advice and does not replace reading the signs and markings on the street.
  2. The signs on the street prevail. Every sign, curb marking, temporary sign or instruction from a parking inspector or police officer prevails over the information in the app. Checking the signs and parking legally is the driver's responsibility alone.
  3. The information may be wrong, incomplete or out of date. The map is built from the Tel Aviv-Yafo Municipality's open data, from OpenStreetMap and from user reports, and in the trial version some street-level parking arrangements are simulated. The app does not know about temporary signs, roadworks, events, road closures, holiday arrangements or changes the municipality has not yet published.
  4. The colors are an estimate based on what you entered. "Free", "allowed" and "paid" are worked out from the permit zone, vehicle type and entitlements you chose, and from the time and length of stay. It is your responsibility that these details are correct and that your permit or badge is valid.
  5. Reminders. A reminder before parking stops being allowed is only an aid, and it may not arrive (phone off, notifications blocked, a fault). Don't rely on it.
  6. Rates. Rates and paid hours are shown according to the published municipal policy and may change. Parking itself is paid in the official parking apps, not in Panui.
  7. Reports. An error report helps us fix the map. We check it before changing anything, and we don't promise to make a fix, or to make it by a certain time.

Handing over spots

  1. Panui only passes on information. It lets people know that a driver is about to leave a spot on a public street. The spot does not belong to the driver leaving, to Panui or to the receiver, and it is not sold. There is no guarantee that the spot will be free, legal, large enough or allowed for your vehicle. Before parking, check the signs as you would for any spot.
  2. "Held for you" in the app is not a right to the spot. Holding only decides to whom the vacancy is shown and who is invited to come. It gives no right over another driver who got to the spot first, and you must not argue or get into a confrontation over a spot.
  3. No payment between users. Asking for or giving money or any benefit for a spot is not allowed. Anyone who does so may lose their account.
  4. Don't block the road or hold a spot against the law. Don't stop in a lane, double-park or stand in a spot to "save" it. The driver leaving does not have to stay after the time they published, and can cancel in the app.
  5. Prohibited behavior. Publishing a vacancy that does not exist, claiming without meaning to come, using several accounts or another device to earn points, and false reports. In such cases Panui may hold points, limit or close the account.

Meeting other users

  1. Meeting strangers. A handoff is a short meeting on the street between people who don't know each other. Panui does not check users' identity, license or background, and is not a party to the meeting.
  2. Age and license. Handing over spots is meant for drivers with a valid driving license, aged 18 or over.
  3. Look after yourself. Contact stays inside the app: there is no need to exchange phone numbers or personal details, and your name and email are not shown. If something feels unsafe, you can stay in your car, give up the handoff and leave. Giving up for safety reasons won't be held against you when we review the report.
  4. No confrontations. Threats, abuse, harassment and force are not allowed. Report abusive behavior from the app, and you can block the user. In immediate danger, call the police at 100.
  5. Responsibility for the meeting. Each user is responsible for their own behavior. Panui is not liable for damage, injury or a dispute arising from a meeting between users, to the extent the law allows.

Safety and driving

  1. No phone use while driving. The law forbids holding a phone while driving. When the phone is moving, Panui asks whether you are driving or a passenger before typing, claiming, publishing or reporting. Answering "passenger" while driving breaks these terms and is your responsibility.
  2. Press "Handoff" only when the car is standing where stopping is allowed. In a car with CarPlay, use the car display, which is designed for driving.

Points, tiers and Premium

  1. Points are not money. They have no monetary value, cannot be redeemed or transferred, and are used only to decide the order in which vacancies are seen. Points can't be bought. Panui may change the rules for points and tiers and will announce a significant change in advance.
  2. Held points. Points earned from a handoff unlock after 24 hours. A handoff with unusual signals is held for review.
  3. Premium. Premium removes ads, and that's all: it doesn't change the order in which vacancies are seen or the hold time. In the trial version it is free and for testing only. Once it costs money, buying and cancelling will be done through the App Store or Google Play, and the price, billing period and renewal will be shown before you buy.
  4. Ads. You can watch an ad to move up a tier for a limited time. In the trial version a sample ad is shown.
